**Short answer:** a **TFN (Tax File Number)** is *you* — your personal ID with the tax office, for life. An **ABN (Australian Business Number)** is *your business* — the number you put on invoices when you work for yourself. Most people working in Australia have a TFN. You only need an ABN when you're running your own show.

If you're cleaning houses, driving deliveries, doing trades, or invoicing clients under your own name, you almost certainly need **both**. Here's how they actually work.

## What a TFN is

Your TFN is a personal number from the ATO. You get one and keep it forever, even if you leave and come back.

- You give it to an **employer** so they don't tax you at the highest rate.
- You give it to your **bank** so your interest isn't over-taxed.
- You use it to **lodge your tax return** every year.

You never put your TFN on an invoice and you never give it to a client. It's private.

## What an ABN is

An ABN identifies your **business** to the government and to the people who pay you. You need one when you work for yourself instead of (or as well as) having an employer.

- It goes **on every invoice** you send.
- It tells clients you're a legitimate business, not an employee.
- It's how the ATO links your business income to you.

Getting an ABN is **free** through the official [Australian Business Register](https://www.abr.gov.au/). If a website charges you for "ABN registration", you're paying for a form you can fill in yourself in ten minutes.

> **Myth, busted:** you do **not** need to earn $75,000 to get an ABN. That $75k figure is the **GST** threshold — a different thing entirely. You can (and should) get an ABN from your very first paid job as a sole trader, no matter how little you earn.

## The trap: no ABN on your invoice = taxed at 47%

This is the part nobody warns you about. If you invoice a business **without** a valid ABN, the payer is legally required to withhold **47%** of your payment and send it to the ATO. You'd eventually claim it back at tax time — but you'd be without nearly half your money for months.

That's the single most expensive mistake a new sole trader makes. An ABN on the invoice avoids it completely.

## So which do I need?

| You're... | TFN | ABN |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| An employee (boss takes out tax) | ✅ | ❌ |
| A sole trader / contractor invoicing clients | ✅ | ✅ |
| Doing both a casual job *and* side work | ✅ | ✅ |
| An international student working casual | ✅ | only if you also invoice |

If you only ever work for an employer, a TFN is enough. The moment you send your first invoice, you need an ABN too.

## The bit that bites later

With a **TFN job**, your employer takes the tax out before you're paid — it's handled. With an **ABN**, nobody takes anything out. You get the full amount, spend it, and then in July the ATO wants its share. The number-one shock we see from sole traders is the tax bill they didn't put aside for.
