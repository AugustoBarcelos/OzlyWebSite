**TFN = you. ABN = your business.** A Tax File Number is your personal ID with the tax office, for life. An Australian Business Number identifies the business you run when you work for yourself. Most people in Australia have a TFN; you only add an ABN when you start invoicing your own clients.

If you clean houses, do trades, drive deliveries, or send invoices under your own name, you almost certainly need **both** — and getting that wrong can cost you nearly half a payment. Here's exactly how they work.

## What a TFN is

Your TFN is a private number issued by the ATO. You get it once and keep it for life, even if you leave the country and come back.

- Give it to your **employer** so you're not taxed at the highest rate.
- Give it to your **bank** so your interest isn't over-taxed.
- Use it to **lodge your tax return** every year.

You never put your TFN on an invoice and never hand it to a client. It stays private.

## What an ABN is

An ABN identifies your **business** to the government and to whoever pays you. You need one the moment you work for yourself instead of (or alongside) having an employer.

- It goes **on every invoice** you send.
- It signals you're a legitimate business, not an employee.
- It's how the ATO links your business income back to you.

Getting an ABN is **free** through the official [Australian Business Register](https://www.abr.gov.au/). If a website charges you to "register your ABN", you're paying for a ten-minute form you can complete yourself.

> **Myth, busted:** you do **not** need to earn $75,000 to get an ABN. That $75k figure is the **GST** registration threshold — a completely separate thing. You can, and should, get an ABN from your very first paid job as a sole trader, no matter how little you earn.

## The mistake that costs you 47%

Here's the part nobody warns you about. If you invoice a business **without** a valid ABN, the payer is legally required to withhold up to **47%** of your payment and send it straight to the ATO. You'd claim it back eventually at tax time — but you'd be without nearly half your money for months.

It's the single most expensive slip a new sole trader makes, and the fix is trivial: put your ABN on the invoice.

## So which do I need?

| You're… | TFN | ABN |
|---|:---:|:---:|
| An employee (your boss withholds tax) | ✅ | ❌ |
| A sole trader / contractor invoicing clients | ✅ | ✅ |
| Doing a casual job **and** side work | ✅ | ✅ |
| An international student in a casual job | ✅ | only if you also invoice |

If you only ever work for an employer, a TFN is enough. The moment you send your first invoice, you need an ABN too.

## The part that bites in July

With a **TFN job**, your employer takes the tax out before you're paid — it's handled for you. With an **ABN**, nobody withholds anything. You receive the full amount, spend it, and then in July the ATO wants its share. The number-one shock we see from sole traders is a tax bill they never set money aside for.
